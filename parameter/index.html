Overview

Parameter organizes lab results, genetic information, and clinical records into one system that highlights patterns, signals, and actions.

Not to replace medical professionals, but to help people ask them better questions.

Design Challenge

How might health data become easier to interpret and act on?

Problem

Easy to access, hard to understand

Health data is increasingly accessible but still scattered. Few tools say which signals matter, how metrics relate, or what to actually do about them.

Solution

One interface for all personal health records

Signals, trends, and insights instead of dense dashboards. Wearables, lab reports, and medical history consolidate into one interface, revealed progressively.

AI summaries surface the relationships between metrics, so what matters reads at a glance.

Approach

Navigating complex systems

How people currently meet their health information: clinical portals, lab reports, consumer apps.

Approach

Fragmented clinical workflows

Clinical environments run on legacy machines, fixed terminals, paper, and isolated databases, so patients and providers both piece records together by hand.

User Research

Studying how people currently manage health data

How health information is collected, stored, and experienced across clinical and consumer systems.

Results arrive without explanation of how metrics relate or what they mean for a given life. The challenge is turning records into information people can use.

Ideation

Validating early concepts

Rapid sketching to test different ways health information could be organized, visualized, and navigated.

These focused on how signals, timelines, and context might appear together, and which arrangements read most clearly.

Industry Audit

Health data lives in separate tools

Most health tools are built around a single type of information: genetics, activity, clinical records, or appointments.

Each works well in its category and rarely talks to the others, leaving people to connect information that would mean more together.

Insights

Access to records is not understanding

Seeing the data was never the hard part. Understanding it is.

Scattered

Health information lives across disconnected systems.

Hard to understand

Results lack context and meaningful explanation.

High friction

Understanding your health requires navigating multiple tools.

Health Data Interface

Viewing health metrics in context

Instead of isolated numbers, each metric sits in a timeline so change over time is visible.

Plain-language explanations cover what each result represents and why it may matter.

Data Visualization

Bringing multiple lab results into one view

A single view for scanning key indicators: cholesterol, inflammation markers, cardiac signals.

Each carries a status indicator, history, and context, so indicators read in relation to each other, not just on their own.

Design Decisions

An interface that responds to changing needs

Health information isn't static, so the interface isn't either. As data arrives the system reorders what it surfaces, prioritizing changes and emerging patterns over a fixed dashboard.

This creates a more flexible environment where the experience evolves alongside a person's health journey.

Product Feature

Document import

Health information arrives as PDFs and scans that resist searching. The document scanner captures them in place.

Extracted and filed alongside existing data, turning static reports into structured history.

Product Feature

AI health assistant

Rather than searching reports or crossing screens, people ask about symptoms, results, or topics directly.

It surfaces what's relevant, explains it, and points toward what's worth looking at next.

Reflection

Design is essential in understanding complex experiences

Interpreting health data is as much a design problem as a technical one. Clarity is the most useful thing an interface can offer.

Designing for complexity

Structure is what carries medical records into something a person can actually understand.

Designing for trust

Health interfaces need more than clean visuals. They need to reduce ambiguity and leave people confident about what to ask next.

Connecting fragmented systems

The opportunity was never another isolated tool. It was connecting records, conversations, and metrics into one coherent experience.
